LL BROWN LLC

PAYMENT TERMS, CREDIT & COLLECTION AGREEMENT

Status: FINAL — ACTIVE
Version: 1.0
Effective Date: 09/18/2026

These Payment Terms, Credit & Collection Agreement ("Payment Terms") are established by LL Brown LLC, an Indiana limited liability company ("LL Brown"), and govern transactions and obligations for which these Payment Terms have been validly incorporated, accepted, or otherwise made applicable.

These Payment Terms are intended to establish clear requirements concerning authorization, invoicing, payment, credit, billing documentation, disputes, progress payments, retainage, late payment, collection, liens, bonds, payment security, electronic communications, and related matters.

ARTICLE 1

DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATION

1.1 LL Brown

"LL Brown" means LL Brown LLC and, where the context requires, its authorized representatives, successors, and permitted assigns.

1.2 Payor

"Payor" means the individual or entity obligated or potentially obligated to make payment to LL Brown under an applicable transaction.

A Payor may include, as applicable:

  • a customer;

  • property owner;

  • general contractor;

  • prime contractor;

  • subcontractor;

  • construction manager;

  • governmental entity;

  • municipality;

  • public agency;

  • business entity;

  • purchaser;

  • lessee;

  • insurer;

  • surety;

  • vendor owing a refund, reimbursement, or credit;

  • individual; or

  • another person or entity having a contractual or legal payment obligation to LL Brown.

1.3 Customer

"Customer" means a person or entity requesting, authorizing, purchasing, receiving, or benefiting from Work provided by LL Brown.

The terms Customer and Payor may refer to different entities.

1.4 Work

"Work" means labor, equipment, services, materials, transportation, mobilization, demobilization, trucking, crane services, rigging, equipment use, rentals, supervision, overtime, standby, change work, extra work, reimbursable costs, or other goods or services furnished by LL Brown.

1.5 Transaction

"Transaction" means any project, job, service request, purchase, rental, contract, subcontract, work order, estimate, proposal, account arrangement, change order, reimbursement obligation, or other matter giving rise to an amount payable to LL Brown.

1.6 Transaction Documents

"Transaction Documents" may include:

  1. these Payment Terms;

  2. an executed contract or subcontract;

  3. accepted estimate or proposal;

  4. purchase order;

  5. work authorization;

  6. work order;

  7. change order;

  8. field ticket;

  9. customer-account agreement;

  10. payment application;

  11. invoice;

  12. written amendment;

  13. applicable plans or specifications;

  14. applicable governmental requirements; and

  15. other documents expressly incorporated into the applicable transaction.

1.7 Invoice

"Invoice" means a written or electronic demand or request for payment issued by LL Brown.

1.8 Invoice Date

"Invoice Date" means the date stated on the applicable LL Brown invoice.

1.9 Due Date

Unless another controlling Transaction Document or mandatory law provides otherwise, the Due Date is the Invoice Date because LL Brown's standard payment term is Due on Receipt.

1.10 Balance

"Balance" means the unpaid amount lawfully owed to LL Brown.

1.11 Undisputed Amount

"Undisputed Amount" means any portion of an invoice or other payment request for which the Payor has not asserted a specific good-faith dispute supported by a reasonably sufficient explanation.

1.12 Disputed Amount

"Disputed Amount" means an amount for which the Payor has timely communicated a specific good-faith dispute concerning entitlement, quantity, rate, scope, performance, calculation, or another material billing issue.

1.13 Applicable Law

"Applicable Law" means all statutes, regulations, ordinances, rules, binding governmental requirements, and controlling legal requirements applicable to the transaction.

1.14 Upstream Payor

"Upstream Payor" means an owner, contractor, public entity, insurer, surety, or other person or entity from whom a Payor expects to receive money associated with LL Brown's Work.

1.15 Written or In Writing

"Written" or "in writing" includes paper and legally recognized electronic communications and electronic records.

1.16 Calendar Day

"Calendar Day" means every day of the calendar unless these Payment Terms expressly refer to a Business Day.

1.17 Business Day

"Business Day" means a day other than Saturday, Sunday, or a legal holiday applicable to the transaction.

AGREEMENT AND SCOPE

2.1 Purpose

These Payment Terms establish LL Brown's standard commercial payment requirements.

2.2 Transactions Covered

These Payment Terms may apply to amounts owed for:

  • crane and equipment services;

  • labor;

  • equipment rental or use;

  • trucking;

  • mobilization and demobilization;

  • fuel surcharges;

  • overtime;

  • standby;

  • rigging;

  • materials;

  • subcontract work;

  • public or private construction work;

  • progress-billed work;

  • change orders;

  • reimbursable costs;

  • retainage;

  • refunds or reimbursements owed to LL Brown;

  • credits owed to LL Brown;

  • overpayments to another party that must be returned;

  • collection costs where recoverable;

  • finance charges where applicable; and

  • other lawful obligations payable to LL Brown.

2.3 Existing Debts

Providing these Payment Terms after an obligation has already arisen does not, by itself, retroactively create a new contractual finance charge, attorney-fee obligation, collection charge, guaranty, or other material term that was not previously part of the transaction.

For such an existing debt, these Payment Terms may serve as LL Brown's payment-policy and collection notice while the original agreement and Applicable Law continue to determine the parties' legal rights.

ARTICLE 3

ACCEPTANCE AND FORMATION

3.1 Methods of Acceptance

Where legally sufficient, a Payor or Customer may accept these Payment Terms through:

(a) handwritten signature;

(b) electronic signature;

(c) electronic acceptance of an estimate or proposal;

(d) written email acceptance;

(e) written text-message acceptance;

(f) issuance of a purchase order after receiving these Payment Terms;

(g) authorization to schedule the Work after receiving these Payment Terms;

(h) authorization or direction to proceed with Work after receiving these Payment Terms;

(i) a customer-account agreement incorporating these Payment Terms; or

(j) another legally sufficient method demonstrating agreement.

3.2 Authorization to Proceed

Where LL Brown provides these Payment Terms before Work is authorized, and Applicable Law permits acceptance through conduct, authorization to proceed after receipt of the Payment Terms may evidence acceptance.

3.3 Repeat Customers

LL Brown may establish a customer account under which these Payment Terms apply to future transactions unless:

  • LL Brown and the Payor expressly agree otherwise in writing; or

  • another controlling agreement supersedes a particular provision.

3.4 Authority of Representative

A person accepting these Payment Terms on behalf of a business represents that the person has authority to authorize the applicable transaction on behalf of that business.

This provision does not create personal liability for the representative.

3.5 Electronic Transactions

Electronic records, signatures, approvals, purchase orders, estimates, work authorizations, and other electronic communications may be used to form and document transactions to the extent permitted by Applicable Law.

3.6 Version Applicable

The version furnished or incorporated when the transaction is entered into governs that transaction unless the parties subsequently agree to a valid modification.

ARTICLE 4

TRANSACTION DOCUMENTS AND ORDER OF PRECEDENCE

4.1 Integrated Transaction

The applicable Transaction Documents collectively establish the parties' agreement concerning the transaction.

4.2 Specific Written Agreements

A separately negotiated, accepted, or executed written contract, subcontract, purchase order, or other controlling written agreement controls over these general Payment Terms to the extent of a direct conflict.

4.3 Mandatory Law

Mandatory Applicable Law controls over any conflicting contractual provision to the extent the law cannot validly be modified by agreement.

4.4 Remaining Provisions

A conflicting provision does not invalidate unrelated provisions of these Payment Terms.

4.5 Purchase Orders and Customer Forms

Receipt or processing of a Payor's purchase order, vendor form, portal requirement, standard conditions, remittance form, or other boilerplate document does not by itself constitute LL Brown's acceptance of a term that materially modifies LL Brown's payment rights.

4.6 Express Modification Required

A material modification of these Payment Terms must be expressly accepted by an authorized LL Brown representative where Applicable Law requires agreement.

ARTICLE 5

STANDARD PAYMENT TERM

5.1 Due on Receipt

Unless another controlling agreement or mandatory law expressly establishes another payment deadline:

ALL INVOICES ARE DUE ON RECEIPT.

5.2 Immediate Obligation

Payment in full is due immediately upon receipt of the applicable invoice.

5.3 Invoice and Due Date

LL Brown may state the same calendar date as both the Invoice Date and Due Date.

5.4 No Implied Net Terms

A history of LL Brown allowing payment after an invoice date does not create:

  • Net 15;

  • Net 30;

  • Net 45;

  • Net 60;

  • end-of-month terms; or

  • another implied credit term.

5.5 Grace Period Does Not Modify Due Date

Any grace period provided before a finance charge begins does not modify the Due on Receipt payment term.

5.6 Different Agreed Terms

If LL Brown expressly agrees to different payment terms for a particular transaction, those terms govern that transaction.

ARTICLE 6

CREDIT

6.1 Extension of Credit

Any decision by LL Brown to permit payment after delivery of an invoice constitutes an extension of credit and not a permanent modification of its Due on Receipt policy.

6.2 Credit Is Discretionary

Except where LL Brown is contractually obligated otherwise, LL Brown may determine the credit terms applicable to future transactions.

6.3 Advance Payment or Deposit

For future Work, LL Brown may require:

  • advance payment;

  • a deposit;

  • partial prepayment;

  • payment before mobilization;

  • payment upon completion;

  • satisfactory credit arrangements;

  • a joint check;

  • additional security; or

  • another commercially reasonable payment arrangement.

6.4 Existing Obligations

Modification or withdrawal of future credit privileges does not alter amounts already owed under an existing transaction.

7.3 Administrative Errors

A clerical or administrative error that does not affect the substantive validity of the underlying obligation does not automatically extinguish that obligation.

7.4 Corrected Invoice

LL Brown may issue a corrected invoice where appropriate.

Issuance of a corrected invoice does not automatically restart the payment period for portions of the balance that were previously invoiced correctly, except where Applicable Law or a controlling agreement provides otherwise.

ARTICLE 8

DELIVERY AND RECEIPT OF INVOICES

8.1 Permitted Delivery

Invoices may be delivered by:

(a) email;

(b) electronic billing system;

(c) customer portal;

(d) contractor portal;

(e) governmental portal;

(f) mail;

(g) hand delivery; or

(h) another agreed or commercially reasonable method.

8.2 Payor Billing Contact

The Payor is responsible for providing accurate billing contact information and promptly updating material changes.

8.3 Undeliverable Invoice

If LL Brown learns that an invoice was not successfully delivered through the agreed billing method, LL Brown should reasonably attempt redelivery.

8.4 Internal Processing

The Payor's internal routing, approval, coding, accounting, or payment-processing procedures do not automatically modify LL Brown's agreed payment terms.

ARTICLE 9

PURCHASE ORDERS, PORTALS, AND BILLING INFORMATION

9.1 Payor Responsibility

A Payor requiring a:

  • purchase order;

  • billing portal;

  • job number;

  • cost code;

  • project number;

  • vendor registration;

  • special invoice format;

  • specific billing email;

  • payment application; or

  • other administrative requirement

should disclose that requirement before Work is performed whenever reasonably practicable.

9.2 Purchase Order Delay

Failure by the Payor to timely issue its required PO does not automatically eliminate an otherwise valid obligation for authorized Work.

9.3 Portal Rejection

If an invoice or pay application is rejected through a billing portal, the Payor should promptly provide the reason for rejection and information reasonably necessary to cure the rejection.

9.4 Internal Approval

The Payor's failure to obtain its own internal approval does not, by itself, establish that authorized Work is not payable.

ARTICLE 10

PROGRESS PAYMENTS AND CONSTRUCTION BILLING

10.1 Applicability

This Article applies where the transaction uses progress billing, payment applications, schedules of values, or periodic billing.

10.2 Schedule of Values

When required, LL Brown may provide a Schedule of Values allocating the contract value among applicable portions of the Work.

10.3 Payment Applications

LL Brown may submit progress-payment applications based upon:

  • completed Work;

  • approved changes;

  • materials;

  • equipment;

  • mobilization;

  • retainage;

  • stored materials where permitted; and

  • other properly billable amounts.

10.4 Timely Processing

A Payor responsible for transmitting LL Brown's payment request to an Upstream Payor shall process and submit the approved amount in a timely manner.

10.5 Identification of Submission

Upon reasonable request concerning an overdue progress payment, the Payor should identify:

  • the upstream pay application containing LL Brown's Work;

  • date submitted;

  • amount submitted for LL Brown;

  • amount approved;

  • amount withheld;

  • amount disputed;

  • retainage;

  • current payment status; and

  • any documented reason payment has not been released.

10.6 Copy of Relevant Payment Request

Where legally and contractually permissible, an intermediary Payor should, upon reasonable request, provide LL Brown with the relevant portion of its current upstream payment request or comparable documentation showing amounts attributable to LL Brown's Work.

10.7 Rejected Amounts

If all or part of LL Brown's payment request is rejected, reduced, or withheld, the Payor should reasonably identify the affected amount and stated reason.

10.8 No Silent Omission

An intermediary Payor should not knowingly omit an approved LL Brown payment request from an upstream application without notifying LL Brown of the omission and the reason for it.

ARTICLE 11

THIRD-PARTY AND UPSTREAM PAYMENT

11.1 General Rule

Unless LL Brown expressly accepts an enforceable contrary provision, LL Brown does not assume the risk that an Upstream Payor may fail to pay the Payor.

11.2 No Automatic Pay-if-Paid Condition

No pay-if-paid or condition-precedent payment obligation shall apply merely because the Payor itself expects payment from another person.

Any such provision must arise from a controlling agreement and be enforceable under Applicable Law; no provision here authorizes or preserves a pay-if-paid or condition-precedent clause where Applicable Law prohibits it.

11.3 Statements Concerning Upstream Payment

Statements such as:

  • "we have not been paid";

  • "the owner hasn't paid";

  • "the contractor hasn't paid";

  • "the pay application hasn't been approved"; or

  • "the money hasn't been released"

do not by themselves establish the status of LL Brown's invoice.

11.4 Accepted Upstream-Payment Arrangement

If LL Brown expressly accepts a valid arrangement making actual upstream payment relevant to timing, the Payor shall nevertheless comply with all payment and disclosure duties required by the applicable agreement and Applicable Law.

11.5 Payment After Upstream Receipt

Where payment is contractually tied to upstream receipt and such provision is enforceable, payment attributable to LL Brown should be transmitted within the time required by the controlling agreement or Applicable Law.

ARTICLE 12

RETAINAGE

12.1 No Retainage Unless Authorized

Retainage shall not be withheld from LL Brown unless authorized by:

  • the controlling agreement; or

  • Applicable Law.

12.2 Retainage Records

Applicable records should identify:

  • gross amount earned;

  • retainage percentage;

  • retainage amount;

  • amount released;

  • remaining retainage; and

  • basis for continued withholding.

12.3 Reduction of Retainage

Any required reduction or release of retainage shall be handled in accordance with the controlling agreement and Applicable Law.

12.4 No Hidden Retainage

A deduction functioning as retainage should not be disguised as an unexplained withholding.

ARTICLE 13

LIEN AND CLAIM WAIVERS

13.1 Conditional Waivers

Where permitted by Applicable Law, LL Brown may condition lien, bond, or claim waivers upon actual receipt of payment.

13.2 No Waiver Beyond Payment

No waiver should be construed as releasing more than the amount and rights expressly described in the waiver.

13.3 Unpaid Amounts

Unless expressly and validly waived, a waiver shall not automatically release:

  • unpaid invoices;

  • unpaid retainage;

  • disputed amounts;

  • pending approved changes;

  • amounts expressly reserved; or

  • later-arising obligations.

13.4 Cleared Funds

Where legally permissible, a payment-related waiver is effective only to the extent the corresponding payment is actually received and collected.

ARTICLE 14

CHANGE WORK AND ADDITIONAL AMOUNTS

14.1 Approved Changes

Authorized changes may result in additional amounts owed to LL Brown.

14.2 Documentation

LL Brown may support change work using:

  • written change orders;

  • directives;

  • emails;

  • text communications;

  • field documentation;

  • tickets;

  • time records;

  • equipment records; or

  • other competent evidence.

14.3 No Unilateral Deduction

The Payor may not unilaterally deduct an alleged change, credit, backcharge, or offset without a contractual or legal basis.

14.4 Disputed Changes

Disputed change-order amounts shall be handled pursuant to Article 18.

ARTICLE 15

PAYMENT METHODS

15.1 Permitted Methods

LL Brown may accept payment by methods it makes available, including:

  • check;

  • ACH;

  • electronic payment;

  • wire;

  • credit or debit card where offered; or

  • another approved payment method.

15.2 Payment Instructions

The Payor shall use payment instructions provided through an authorized LL Brown source.

15.3 Cleared Funds

Payment is considered finally received when collected funds are actually received by LL Brown and are no longer subject to ordinary rejection or dishonor.

15.4 Failed Payment

A rejected ACH, dishonored check, failed card transaction, rejected transfer, or other unsuccessful payment does not satisfy the obligation.

15.5 Returned-Payment Costs

The Payor may be responsible for actual returned-payment costs and any additional charge lawfully imposed under Applicable Law and the parties' agreement.

ARTICLE 16

APPLICATION AND EFFECT OF PAYMENTS

16.1 Partial Payment

LL Brown may accept partial payment without waiving the remaining Balance.

16.2 No Automatic Settlement

Acceptance of less than the total Balance does not automatically constitute:

  • accord and satisfaction;

  • settlement;

  • waiver;

  • release; or

  • forgiveness of the remainder.

16.3 Application of Payments

Subject to Applicable Law and any accepted remittance designation, LL Brown may apply payments in a commercially reasonable manner to obligations owing on the applicable account.

16.4 Payment Not Acceptance of Other Claims

Receipt of money does not constitute acceptance of an unrelated backcharge, deduction, claim, or proposed modification.

ARTICLE 17

SETOFFS, DEDUCTIONS, AND BACKCHARGES

17.1 No Unauthorized Setoff

The Payor may not reduce an otherwise payable amount by a unilateral setoff, deduction, or backcharge unless:

(a) LL Brown agrees;

(b) the controlling agreement authorizes it; or

(c) Applicable Law permits it.

17.2 Documentation

A proposed backcharge should identify:

  • amount;

  • reason;

  • date;

  • event giving rise to the charge;

  • contractual basis;

  • supporting records; and

  • calculation.

17.3 Separate Obligations

An unrelated claim against LL Brown does not automatically permit withholding of payment on an otherwise undisputed invoice.

ARTICLE 18

BILLING DISPUTES

18.1 Prompt Notice

A Payor asserting a billing dispute shall notify LL Brown promptly after discovering the alleged issue.

18.2 Required Information

The dispute notice should identify:

  1. invoice number;

  2. specific disputed amount;

  3. specific reason;

  4. relevant supporting documentation;

  5. requested correction or resolution; and

  6. undisputed amount.

18.3 General Statements Insufficient

A general statement that an invoice is:

  • incorrect;

  • pending;

  • waiting for approval;

  • not processed;

  • not yet funded; or

  • unpaid upstream

does not by itself identify a substantive billing dispute.

18.4 Undisputed Amounts

Unless a controlling agreement or Applicable Law provides otherwise, the existence of a dispute concerning one portion does not suspend payment of an Undisputed Amount.

18.5 Good-Faith Review

LL Brown shall review reasonably documented billing disputes in good faith.

18.6 No Admission

Investigation or discussion of a disputed amount does not constitute an admission by LL Brown.

ARTICLE 19

DUE DATE, DELINQUENCY, AND GRACE PERIOD

19.1 Due Date

The standard payment term remains:

DUE ON RECEIPT.

19.2 Past-Due Status

An unpaid amount is past due after it has become payable and remains unpaid.

19.3 Finance-Charge Grace Period

LL Brown provides a fifteen (15) calendar-day grace period following the Invoice Date before the contractual finance charge in Article 20 begins, where that charge is otherwise enforceable.

19.4 Effect of Grace Period

The grace period delays only commencement of the contractual finance charge.

It does not establish Net 15 payment terms.

ARTICLE 20

FINANCE CHARGES AND STATUTORY INTEREST

20.1 Contractual Finance Charge

Where these Payment Terms were validly incorporated into the applicable transaction and Applicable Law permits the charge, unpaid principal remaining fifteen (15) calendar days after the Invoice Date may accrue a finance charge at the lesser of:

one percent (1.0%) per month, equivalent to twelve percent (12%) per annum;

or

the maximum lawful rate.

20.2 Calculation

Unless another method is required by law, the contractual finance charge shall be calculated against unpaid principal and shall not be compounded.

20.3 Statutory Interest

If Applicable Law or a controlling contract grants LL Brown a different interest remedy, LL Brown reserves the right to claim the legally applicable remedy.

20.4 No Retroactive Contractual Charge

LL Brown will not claim that this contractual finance-charge provision was retroactively incorporated into an earlier obligation merely because these Payment Terms were later sent with a collection notice.

ARTICLE 21

COLLECTION COSTS

21.1 Recoverable Costs

Where contractually and legally recoverable, the Payor agrees to reimburse reasonable costs actually incurred by LL Brown to collect a past-due obligation.

21.2 Potential Costs

Recoverable costs may include, where permitted:

  • attorneys' fees;

  • court costs;

  • filing fees;

  • service fees;

  • collection-agency charges;

  • statutory notice costs;

  • certified-mail costs;

  • reasonable lien-filing costs;

  • reasonable bond-claim costs; and

  • other direct collection expenses.

21.3 Applicable-Law Limitation

LL Brown shall not impose a contractual collection charge prohibited by Applicable Law.

ARTICLE 22

COLLECTION PROCEDURES

22.1 Immediate Follow-Up

Because payment is Due on Receipt, LL Brown may begin reasonable payment-status follow-up after payment becomes due.

22.2 Collection Communications

LL Brown may:

  • resend invoices;

  • provide statements;

  • verify receipt;

  • request payment status;

  • request payment dates;

  • request explanations for withholding;

  • contact accounts payable;

  • contact project management;

  • contact management;

  • send formal demand;

  • preserve security rights;

  • restrict future credit; or

  • pursue other lawful remedies.

22.3 Payment Commitment

LL Brown may record a promise to pay, including:

  • person making the commitment;

  • date;

  • amount;

  • invoices;

  • method; and

  • promised date.

22.4 Promise Is Not Payment

A promise that payment will be made does not constitute payment.

ARTICLE 23

CREDIT HOLD AND FUTURE WORK

23.1 Grounds

LL Brown may place future unsecured Work on credit hold based upon:

  • overdue balances;

  • repeated delinquency;

  • broken payment promises;

  • dishonored payment;

  • unresolved payment disputes;

  • material credit deterioration;

  • significant additional exposure; or

  • another reasonable credit concern.

23.2 Future Work

LL Brown may require payment of an outstanding Balance before accepting additional Work unless LL Brown is contractually required to proceed.

23.3 Existing Contract

LL Brown will not suspend an existing contractual obligation except in accordance with the controlling contract and Applicable Law.

ARTICLE 24

DEFAULT AND ADEQUATE ASSURANCE

24.1 Payment Default

Failure to make an undisputed payment when required may constitute a payment default.

24.2 Additional Events

Other credit-related default events may include:

  • dishonored payment;

  • materially false credit information;

  • repudiation of an acknowledged payment obligation;

  • insolvency-related concerns to the extent legally relevant;

  • unauthorized chargeback; or

  • material breach affecting LL Brown's payment security.

24.3 Adequate Assurance

When reasonable grounds for insecurity arise concerning future payment, LL Brown may request reasonable assurance concerning future transactions to the extent permitted by the applicable agreement and law.

24.4 Future Credit

Pending adequate assurance, LL Brown may modify future credit arrangements where legally and contractually permissible.

ARTICLE 25

MECHANIC'S LIENS AND PAYMENT SECURITY

25.1 Reservation

LL Brown expressly reserves every lien, security, and statutory payment right available to it.

25.2 Potential Rights

Such rights may include, where applicable:

  • mechanic's liens;

  • construction liens;

  • public-works claims;

  • payment-bond claims;

  • retainage claims;

  • statutory notices;

  • prompt-payment rights;

  • trust-fund rights;

  • stop-notice remedies where available; and

  • other lawful security rights.

25.3 No Waiver Through Collection Discussions

Ordinary collection discussions, payment extensions, negotiations, reminders, or partial payments do not by themselves waive a lien, bond, or statutory deadline.

ARTICLE 26

PAYMENT BONDS AND SURETIES

26.1 Bond Information

Where a payment bond applies to LL Brown's Work, LL Brown reserves the right to request or obtain legally available bond information.

26.2 Claims

Nothing in these Payment Terms waives LL Brown's right to present a timely claim against an applicable payment bond or surety.

26.3 Direct Collection Efforts

Efforts to collect from the Payor do not automatically waive LL Brown's separate rights against an applicable surety.

ARTICLE 27

PUBLIC WORKS AND GOVERNMENT PROJECTS

27.1 Special Requirements

Public and governmental transactions may be subject to special requirements concerning:

  • payment timing;

  • certified payment applications;

  • retainage;

  • prompt payment;

  • interest;

  • DBE participation;

  • payment bonds;

  • notices;

  • claims;

  • administrative remedies; and

  • government billing systems.

27.2 Mandatory Requirements Control

Mandatory governmental and statutory requirements, including project-specific requirements and valid contract flow-down provisions, control to the extent of a direct conflict.

27.3 Reservation

LL Brown does not waive any payment protection available under an applicable public contract, bond, statute, regulation, or program.

ARTICLE 28

DEADLINES AND PRESERVATION OF CLAIMS

28.1 Independent Deadlines

Contractual and statutory claim deadlines shall be evaluated independently of LL Brown's ordinary collection schedule.

28.2 Negotiations

Negotiations or payment discussions do not automatically extend a:

  • lien deadline;

  • bond deadline;

  • notice deadline;

  • claim deadline;

  • contractual deadline; or

  • limitation period.

28.3 Preservation Action

LL Brown may preserve a lien, bond, or other claim while continuing to negotiate payment.

Preservation of such rights does not necessarily terminate settlement discussions.

ARTICLE 29

RESTRICTIVE ENDORSEMENTS AND PURPORTED SETTLEMENTS

29.1 Restrictive Language

Language such as:

  • "payment in full";

  • "paid in full";

  • "full settlement";

  • "full and final settlement"; or

  • similar restrictive language

does not automatically alter the parties' rights solely because LL Brown receives a payment or remittance document.

29.2 Governing Law

Any alleged accord and satisfaction remains subject to Applicable Law.

ARTICLE 30

PAYMENT FRAUD AND CHANGED PAYMENT INSTRUCTIONS

30.1 Verification

A Payor receiving changed banking or electronic-payment instructions purportedly from LL Brown should independently verify them using a previously known LL Brown contact method.

30.2 Unauthorized Recipient

Payment sent to an unauthorized third party may not discharge the Payor's obligation to LL Brown.

30.3 Responsibility

The legal effect of a fraudulent payment diversion shall be determined by the parties' agreement and Applicable Law.

ARTICLE 31

TAXES

31.1 Applicable Taxes

The Payor shall be responsible for taxes properly chargeable to the transaction except taxes imposed directly upon LL Brown's net income.

31.2 Exemption Documentation

A Payor claiming exemption should timely provide valid exemption documentation.

ARTICLE 32

ASSIGNMENT

32.1 LL Brown

LL Brown may assign a receivable or collection right to the extent permitted by Applicable Law and the controlling agreement.

32.2 Payor

The Payor may not avoid an existing payment obligation by assignment, reorganization, subcontracting, or delegation unless LL Brown validly agrees to release the original obligor.

32.3 Successors

These Payment Terms bind permitted successors and assigns to the extent legally applicable.

ARTICLE 33

PERSONAL LIABILITY AND GUARANTIES

33.1 Representative Capacity

An employee, manager, officer, estimator, project manager, superintendent, purchasing employee, or other representative of a business does not become personally liable solely because that person authorized Work on behalf of the business.

33.2 Personal Guaranty

If LL Brown requires a personal guaranty, it must be separately and clearly established.

33.3 No Implied Guaranty

These general Payment Terms do not themselves create an individual personal guaranty.

ARTICLE 34

NOTICES AND COMMUNICATIONS

34.1 Ordinary Communications

Ordinary billing communications may be sent using the contact information provided for the applicable account or transaction.

34.2 Electronic Notice
